# A2 Grammar + Communicative Functions Node Inventory

**Task:** P0-CUR-004A  
**Version:** v1.0-draft  
**Date:** 2026-09-01  
**Status:** Detailed A2 grammar/function baseline

## 1. Purpose

This document extends the validated A1 graph into A2 Grammar (`GR`) and Communicative Functions (`CF`). A2 is defined as a routine communicator who can handle simple direct exchanges, short social interactions, common transactions, and simple descriptions of background/immediate needs.

The CEFR A2 boundary is used for communicative outcomes; the exact grammar sequence below is product-designed and informed by learner-development references rather than presented as an official CEFR syllabus.

# 4. Recommended A2 progression waves

## A2.1 — Past events and background

- past `be`;
- past simple;
- common irregular verbs;
- past questions;
- time-sequence linking;
- short narration.

## A2.2 — Plans, arrangements and invitations

- `going to`;
- present continuous for arrangements;
- introductory `will`;
- appointment/invitation functions.

## A2.3 — Quantity, shopping and services

- count/non-count;
- quantity expressions;
- expanded article control;
- polite requests;
- returns/restaurant/service interactions.

## A2.4 — Comparison, advice and rules

- comparatives/superlatives;
- `too/enough`;
- `should`;
- `have to/must`;
- recommendations and simple rules.

## A2.5 — Longer simple messages

- relative clauses;
- `when/before/after`;
- cause/result;
- simple `if`;
- routine conversation maintenance.

## A2.6 — Experience and integration

- introductory present perfect;
- experience talk;
- integrated A2 transactions/narratives/plans;
- repeated use of clarification and confirmation strategies.

# 5. A2 boundary decisions

Primarily deferred to B1 or later:

- systematic present-perfect vs past-simple contrast;
- present perfect with `since/for`, `already/yet/just` as a full system;
- second conditional as a productive system;
- common passive voice system;
- reported speech system;
- indirect questions as a systematic grammar target;
- expanded gerund/infinitive contrasts;
- broad modal deduction/possibility nuance;
- past perfect narrative system;
- sustained argument/opinion development.
