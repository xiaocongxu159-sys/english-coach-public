# A1–B2 Top-Level Curriculum Tree

**Task:** P0-CUR-001  
**Version:** v1.0-draft  
**Date:** 2026-09-01  
**Status:** Top-level taxonomy baseline; detailed node inventory pending

## 1. Scope

This document defines the **top-level curriculum taxonomy** for A1, A2, B1, and B2.

It does not yet enumerate every teachable knowledge node. Its purpose is to establish:

- what each level is trying to achieve;
- which curriculum families exist at each level;
- how Grammar, Vocabulary, Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing, Pronunciation, and Communicative Functions progress together;
- where real-world communication and IELTS preparation connect to the general-English curriculum.

The hierarchy is product-designed using CEFR communicative outcomes as the proficiency backbone and English Profile research as a grammar/vocabulary reference. IELTS is an application/assessment layer rather than the organizing syllabus.

## 2. Cross-level progression model

```text
A1 — survive and participate in very simple, predictable communication
  ↓
A2 — handle routine everyday exchanges and short connected messages
  ↓
B1 — function independently in familiar life/work situations and sustain connected communication
  ↓
B2 — communicate with greater fluency, precision, range, argument, inference, and interaction
```

The curriculum is not seven independent subject lists. A lesson should increasingly integrate multiple domains.

# 3. A1 — Foundation User

## 3.1 Level outcome

A1 focuses on establishing a reliable core for highly familiar situations. Learners should begin to understand and produce very simple language about themselves, other people, immediate needs, time, place, quantity, routine activities, and basic transactions when support is available.

## 3.2 Grammar families

- **Core sentence architecture**
  - subject + verb + complement/object
  - basic word order
  - affirmative / negative / question distinction
- **Be**
  - present forms
  - identity, description, location
- **Have / have got**
  - possession and basic description
- **Present simple**
  - routines, facts, likes/dislikes
- **Present continuous — core use**
  - actions happening now
- **Imperatives**
  - simple instructions and requests
- **Nouns and determiners**
  - singular/plural
  - a/an
  - basic the exposure/use
  - this/that/these/those
- **Pronouns and possessives**
  - subject/object pronouns
  - possessive adjectives
- **Basic adjectives/adverbs**
  - common adjective position
  - very + adjective
- **Basic prepositions**
  - place
  - time
  - movement in common fixed uses
- **Can/can't**
  - ability, simple permission/request
- **There is/are**
- **Basic wh- and yes/no questions**
- **Basic coordination**
  - and / but / or / because in simple use
- **Past and future awareness**
  - limited lexical/formulaic exposure before full A2 development

## 3.3 Vocabulary & lexical-chunk families

- personal information
- countries, nationalities, languages
- family and people
- numbers, dates, time, prices
- home and everyday objects
- food and drink
- daily routines
- basic work/study identity
- places in town
- transport basics
- clothes and shopping basics
- weather basics
- body and simple health needs
- common action verbs
- basic adjectives for size, quality, feeling, appearance
- social formulas
  - greetings
  - thanks
  - apologies
  - please / excuse me
- survival chunks
  - I'd like ...
  - Can I ...?
  - Where is ...?
  - How much is ...?
  - I don't understand.
  - Could you repeat that? (formulaic support)

## 3.4 Listening families

- recognize names, letters, numbers and spelled information
- identify time, date, price and quantity
- understand very short instructions
- identify key detail in predictable exchanges
- understand basic personal information
- recognize common social formulas
- follow very short descriptions when vocabulary is familiar
- discriminate common high-value words in slow/clear speech

## 3.5 Speaking families

- introduce self and another person
- give basic personal details
- ask/answer simple factual questions
- describe immediate surroundings in short phrases/sentences
- talk briefly about routines and preferences
- order/request simple goods and services
- ask for basic directions/help
- use repair phrases when communication breaks down
- produce short prepared descriptions

## 3.6 Reading families

- recognize common signs and labels
- understand short forms, menus, schedules and simple notices
- extract names, times, prices and basic details
- understand very short personal messages
- follow simple sentence-level descriptions
- use headings/pictures/layout as support

## 3.7 Writing families

- enter personal information in forms
- write isolated phrases and simple sentences
- write a short personal message
- describe self/home/routine in basic sentences
- use basic sentence capitalization and end punctuation

## 3.8 Pronunciation families

- alphabet and spelling clarity
- high-value sound contrasts affecting intelligibility
- plural and common verb ending awareness
- basic word stress in frequent vocabulary
- sentence stress in short statements/questions
- intelligible numbers, dates and prices
- basic rising/falling question intonation awareness

## 3.9 Communicative functions

- greet and close
- identify and describe
- request and respond
- express likes/dislikes
- ask for repetition/clarification
- give simple instructions
- ask/give location
- make a simple purchase/order
- say what one can/cannot do

## 3.10 IELTS relationship

A1 is **pre-IELTS foundation**. Do not prioritize IELTS task drilling. Build listening accuracy, basic question-response ability, spelling/numbers, sentence formation, and confidence producing comprehensible English.

# 4. A2 — Routine Communicator

## 4.1 Level outcome

A2 develops reliable communication in routine daily situations. Learners should handle common transactions, describe personal history and surroundings, understand short connected messages, and begin linking ideas beyond isolated sentences.

## 4.2 Grammar families

- **Past simple**
  - regular/irregular verbs
  - affirmative/negative/questions
- **Past continuous — introductory use**
- **Future forms — common uses**
  - going to for plans/intention
  - will for simple predictions/decisions
  - present continuous for arrangements
- **Present perfect — early/common exposure**
  - basic experience/result uses where developmentally appropriate
- **Comparatives and superlatives**
- **Countability and quantity**
  - some/any
  - much/many
  - a lot of
  - a few/a little
- **Articles — expanding control**
  - a/an/the/zero article in frequent contexts
- **Modals and semi-modals**
  - should
  - have to
  - must/mustn't in common meanings
  - could for polite requests/ability reference
- **Infinitive and -ing patterns — core high-frequency uses**
- **Adverbs of frequency/manner/degree — expansion**
- **Basic relative clauses**
  - who/which/that in simple defining use
- **Basic conditionals**
  - zero/first conditional core meanings
- **Linking**
  - because / so / when / before / after / if
- **Question expansion**
  - more flexible wh- questions
  - simple subject/object question contrast

## 4.3 Vocabulary & lexical-chunk families

- travel and accommodation
- appointments and schedules
- shopping and returns
- restaurants and services
- directions and transport
- health and common symptoms
- work routines and responsibilities
- education and learning
- leisure and entertainment
- technology basics
- household problems
- past experiences and events
- plans and intentions
- feelings and opinions
- comparison and preference
- common phrasal verbs in concrete meanings
- common collocations for routine life
- polite transactional chunks

## 4.4 Listening families

- follow short routine conversations
- identify main topic and explicit details
- follow sequences of events/actions
- understand simple reasons and explanations
- understand common service/transaction language
- follow short announcements/instructions
- recognize paraphrase at a basic level
- use context to recover missed words
- distinguish present/past/future reference in familiar contexts

## 4.5 Speaking families

- narrate a short past event
- describe plans and arrangements
- compare familiar options
- explain simple preferences with reasons
- handle routine service interactions
- describe a problem and ask for help
- give simple directions/instructions
- maintain a short conversation with follow-up questions
- repair misunderstanding with basic strategies
- give a short connected description of a familiar topic

## 4.6 Reading families

- understand short emails/messages
- understand routine instructions
- locate specific information in web pages/notices/timetables
- understand short narratives
- identify main topic and supporting details
- infer simple word meaning from immediate context
- follow basic connectors and sequence markers

## 4.7 Writing families

- write short connected personal messages/emails
- describe a past event
- describe plans and arrangements
- write a simple comparison or preference with reasons
- give written directions/instructions
- use basic paragraph grouping
- increase control of common punctuation and connectors

## 4.8 Pronunciation families

- common consonant/vowel contrasts affecting intelligibility
- past-tense `-ed` awareness/production
- plural/third-person `-s` patterns
- multisyllabic word stress
- basic weak forms in frequent function words
- sentence stress for key information
- chunking short connected speech
- intonation for polite requests, confirmation and simple attitude

## 4.9 Communicative functions

- narrate
- plan and arrange
- compare
- express preference and simple reasons
- advise in common situations
- request politely
- complain about a simple problem
- apologize/explain
- invite/accept/refuse
- check understanding
- give simple recommendations

## 4.10 IELTS relationship

A2 remains mostly foundation, but the system may introduce **micro-skills** used later in IELTS:

- listening for spelling, numbers and form-completion details;
- identifying explicit detail;
- giving 2–4 sentence spoken answers;
- simple personal-topic responses;
- short descriptive writing.

Do not present A2 practice as full IELTS simulation.

# 5. B1 — Independent Communicator

## 5.1 Level outcome

B1 develops independent functioning in familiar social, travel, study, and work contexts. Learners should sustain connected discourse, explain experiences and opinions, follow main points of clear standard speech/text, and manage many everyday communication problems without constant support.

## 5.2 Grammar families

- **Present perfect — systematic development**
  - experience
  - unfinished time / since-for
  - already/yet/just
  - contrast with past simple
- **Past narrative system**
  - past simple / past continuous
  - introductory past perfect where useful
- **Future meanings and contrasts**
  - plans, arrangements, predictions, intentions
- **Conditionals**
  - first conditional consolidation
  - second conditional core hypothetical use
- **Modals**
  - obligation, possibility, deduction, advice, permission
  - must / have to / should / might / may / could
- **Passive voice — common present/past uses**
- **Reported speech — introductory/common patterns**
- **Relative clauses — expanded defining use and introductory non-defining use**
- **Gerund/infinitive patterns — broader high-frequency control**
- **Indirect questions**
- **Quantifiers and determiners — greater precision**
- **Articles — systematic high-frequency contrasts**
- **Complex sentences**
  - although / while / unless / as soon as / until / however at discourse level
- **Purpose/reason/result structures**
- **Comparison and degree — expanded forms**

## 5.3 Vocabulary & lexical-chunk families

- experiences and life changes
- work tasks, performance and problems
- meetings and workplace communication
- learning and education
- travel problem-solving
- relationships and social situations
- media and entertainment
- technology and online life
- health and lifestyle
- money and everyday finance
- environment and community
- describing change/trends at a basic-intermediate level
- explaining cause, result and purpose
- opinion and agreement/disagreement
- problem/solution language
- common abstract adjectives and verbs
- productive collocations
- common phrasal verbs across concrete and semi-abstract meanings
- discourse chunks for extending answers

## 5.4 Listening families

- identify main idea in clear standard speech
- capture important supporting details
- follow reasons, examples and sequence
- follow short interviews and workplace discussions
- distinguish fact from speaker opinion in clear contexts
- recognize common paraphrase/synonymy
- infer likely meaning using context and tone when reasonably explicit
- track pronoun/reference links in short discourse
- understand common reductions/connected speech at moderate natural speed
- take simple notes from short informative speech

## 5.5 Speaking families

- sustain conversation on familiar topics
- narrate experiences with sequence and detail
- explain reasons and consequences
- express and support an opinion
- compare alternatives and make a recommendation
- explain a work/process problem
- participate in a simple meeting/discussion
- ask effective follow-up questions
- clarify, reformulate and repair communication
- give a structured 1–2 minute talk on a familiar topic
- summarize a simple text/conversation in own words

## 5.6 Reading families

- identify main idea and supporting detail in longer everyday texts
- follow argument/reason structure when clearly signaled
- understand workplace and informational articles
- infer vocabulary meaning from context
- track reference and cohesion
- distinguish key information from examples
- scan efficiently for specific information
- summarize central points
- compare information across short texts

## 5.7 Writing families

- write connected multi-paragraph personal/work messages
- narrate and describe experiences
- explain reasons/results
- write a supported opinion paragraph
- compare options
- summarize straightforward information
- organize ideas with topic/support structure
- use a wider range of connectors without excessive repetition
- revise common grammar/clarity errors

## 5.8 Pronunciation families

- consistent word stress in common B1 vocabulary
- sentence stress for focus/new information
- weak forms and reductions in frequent connected speech
- linking and boundary awareness
- chunking longer utterances
- intonation for interest, uncertainty, agreement/disagreement and completion
- intelligible consonant clusters and grammatical endings
- self-monitoring of recurring L1-influenced intelligibility problems

## 5.9 Communicative functions

- explain and justify
- narrate in detail
- compare and recommend
- express agreement/disagreement politely
- speculate with limited certainty
- solve routine problems collaboratively
- summarize
- clarify/rephrase
- make suggestions and respond
- discuss advantages/disadvantages at a basic level
- express cause/consequence

## 5.10 IELTS relationship

B1 begins a deliberate IELTS bridge while general English remains primary.

Relevant applications:

- Speaking Part 1: fuller, natural answers rather than one-sentence replies;
- Speaking Part 2: structured 1–2 minute familiar-topic talk with support;
- early Speaking Part 3: simple opinion + reason + example;
- Listening: main idea/detail/paraphrase/note-taking foundations;
- Reading: skimming/scanning/reference/context inference;
- Writing: paragraph control, description, comparison, reason/example structure.

Official-style full timed assessment remains a checkpoint rather than everyday curriculum.

# 6. B2 — Effective Independent Communicator

## 6.1 Level outcome

B2 develops more fluent and flexible communication across familiar and moderately complex topics. Learners should follow extended discourse, interact without excessive strain, present and defend viewpoints, understand implied relationships and a wider range of text types, and produce clear, organized spoken and written language.

## 6.2 Grammar families

- **Tense/aspect integration**
  - flexible present/past/future reference
  - present perfect continuous
  - past perfect in narrative/explanation
  - higher-control aspect contrasts
- **Conditionals and hypothetical meaning**
  - second conditional consolidation
  - third conditional core use
  - mixed/hybrid meanings where useful
- **Modal meaning — nuance**
  - degrees of certainty
  - past deduction/speculation
  - obligation/advice nuance
- **Passive voice — expanded forms/functions**
- **Reported speech — expanded tense/reporting patterns**
- **Relative clauses — greater control and variety**
- **Participle/reduced structures — selected high-value uses**
- **Advanced comparison/degree**
- **Complex noun phrases — controlled expansion**
- **Cleft/emphasis and inversion — limited high-value awareness/use where appropriate**
- **Discourse grammar**
  - concession
  - contrast
  - condition
  - cause/effect
  - stance
- **Article/determiner precision in broader contexts**
- **Verb-pattern and preposition control**
- **Complex question/reporting structures**

The detailed tree must avoid teaching rare structures merely because they are traditionally listed in grammar books. Frequency, communicative value, and developmental readiness remain selection criteria.

## 6.3 Vocabulary & lexical-chunk families

- nuanced opinion and stance
- argument and evidence
- cause/effect/consequence
- uncertainty and hedging
- evaluation and judgment
- agreement/disagreement with nuance
- work strategy, performance and decision-making
- business discussion and negotiation foundations
- society and public issues
- science/technology/general academic topics
- environment and sustainability
- culture and media
- education and learning systems
- health/well-being at more abstract level
- trends, data and change
- advantages/disadvantages and trade-offs
- abstract nouns and productive word families
- broader phrasal verbs
- collocations for discussion and writing
- register-sensitive alternatives
- discourse markers used naturally rather than mechanically

## 6.4 Listening families

- follow extended discussion with clear organization
- identify speaker stance and degree of certainty
- infer implied meaning when context supports it
- distinguish main argument, support, example and qualification
- follow multiple speakers and track viewpoints
- recognize broader paraphrase and reformulation
- follow moderately dense informational/academic-style speech
- take structured notes
- tolerate unknown vocabulary without losing overall meaning
- understand natural connected speech across a controlled range of accents

## 6.5 Speaking families

- sustain fluent discussion with limited unnatural pausing
- develop an answer with claim, reason, example and qualification
- defend a viewpoint and respond to challenge
- compare complex alternatives/trade-offs
- speculate and hedge appropriately
- participate actively in meetings/discussions
- explain technical/work concepts to a non-expert at a general level
- summarize and synthesize information
- reformulate when exact vocabulary is unavailable
- give a clear multi-minute presentation/talk
- manage turn-taking and conversational nuance

## 6.6 Reading families

- identify thesis/position and argument structure
- distinguish claims, reasons, evidence, examples and qualifications
- infer writer attitude/stance where signaled
- understand broader lexical paraphrase
- interpret reference/cohesion across longer texts
- compare viewpoints across texts
- synthesize information from multiple sections/sources
- read longer informational and semi-academic texts efficiently
- distinguish explicit information from inference
- tolerate unknown vocabulary through contextual reasoning

## 6.7 Writing families

- produce clear multi-paragraph structured texts
- develop and support an argument
- compare/contrast with evaluation
- discuss advantages/disadvantages and trade-offs
- explain cause/effect and problem/solution
- summarize/synthesize source information in original wording
- use appropriate register for common professional contexts
- vary sentence structure while maintaining accuracy
- improve cohesion beyond repetitive linking words
- edit for grammar, lexical precision and clarity

## 6.8 Pronunciation families

- reliable sentence stress and focus placement
- flexible chunking of longer speech
- connected-speech perception and controlled production
- intonation to signal stance, contrast, uncertainty and turn management
- reduced function words without loss of intelligibility
- rhythm supporting fluent delivery
- repair of persistent sound/stress patterns that cause misunderstanding
- accent tolerance in listening

## 6.9 Communicative functions

- argue and defend
- qualify/hedge
- speculate
- evaluate evidence/options
- negotiate and seek compromise
- synthesize information
- present a structured case
- challenge/respond politely
- explain complex cause/effect
- discuss trade-offs
- mediate or re-express information for another person
- manage extended interaction

## 6.10 IELTS relationship

B2 is the main IELTS-readiness development band for the project's initial target.

Applications include:

- Speaking Parts 1–3 with sustained development, flexible language and interaction;
- Listening across all four IELTS-style sections using original simulations and official checkpoints;
- Reading task families including main idea, detail, matching, reference, paraphrase and inference skills;
- Writing Task 1/Task 2 skill development where applicable, especially organization, comparison, overview, argument, support, grammar range and lexical precision;
- timed official-material checkpoints separated from normal learning content.

Internal readiness estimates must remain explicitly non-official.

# 7. Cross-domain dependency principles

## 7.1 Grammar does not own communication

Speaking or writing abilities should not be represented as mere lists of grammar structures. A speaking node may depend on several grammar and lexical nodes but has its own communicative objective.

Example:

```text
GR-B1-COMP-COMPARATIVE-XX
VX-B1-OPINION-PREFERENCE-XX
CF-B1-RECOMMEND-XX
        ↓
SP-B1-COMPARE-RECOMMEND-XX
```

## 7.2 Listening/reading are operation-driven

A comprehension node should primarily test an operation such as identifying detail, following argument, or inferring stance. Topic and genre are separate metadata so the same skill can transfer.

## 7.3 Pronunciation integrates with listening and speaking

Pronunciation training should include both perception and production where relevant. The target is intelligibility and comprehension, not native-accent imitation.

## 7.4 Communicative functions bridge domains

Functions such as requesting, comparing, explaining, disagreeing, and speculating act as bridges among grammar, vocabulary, listening, speaking and writing.

# 8. Content-lane model

Every level may draw scenarios from multiple lanes so systematic learning does not become repetitive.

### Daily life

home, food, shopping, transport, health, relationships, leisure, services

### Work & business

roles, schedules, tasks, problems, meetings, performance, customers, decisions, negotiation foundations

### Travel & international communication

booking, transport, accommodation, directions, disruptions, social interaction

### Information & ideas

technology, environment, education, society, media, general science, culture

### Personal development

habits, goals, learning, experiences, opinions, decisions

The lane must not determine CEFR level by itself. Difficulty comes from language, task, discourse and cognitive demands.

# 10. What this deliverable does not claim

This document does **not** claim that:

- every listed grammar family is exclusive to one CEFR level;
- CEFR itself mandates this teaching order;
- English Grammar Profile is a prescriptive syllabus;
- a learner must master every topic vocabulary item before advancing;
- B2 automatically equals a specific IELTS band score.

It is a transparent product curriculum baseline that will be refined through detailed source checking, assessment design, learner performance data, and later review.
